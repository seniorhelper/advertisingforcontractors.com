Why is my business not showing up on Google Maps?

Before assuming a penalty, check the boring explanation first. The most common cause by a distance is proximity — you were never going to rank in a suburb eight miles away, and no amount of work or budget changes that.

Reading time 8 min Published August 15, 2026 Sources named and dated
In this article

  2. 2. Checking it wrong
  3. 3–5. Profile problems

1. Proximity \u2014 the answer most of the time

Map pack rankings are driven heavily by how close your registered business address is to the person searching. Your visibility radiates outward from that address, which means a contractor eight miles from a suburb will struggle to appear there regardless of how good their profile is.

Setting a wider service area in your Google Business Profile does not change this. It tells Google where you are willing to work; it does not extend where you rank. This is the single most expensive misunderstanding in contractor local marketing, because contractors spend months optimising a profile for towns they were never going to reach.

What actually extends reach is a mix of things that take time: organic service-area pages that can rank without proximity, review volume and prominence that let you outrank closer but weaker competitors, and paid channels that ignore geography constraints entirely. If a suburb genuinely matters commercially, a second physical location is the honest answer, with everything that implies.

2. You are checking from the wrong place

Searching from your own shop tells you almost nothing, because you are the closest business to yourself. Searching while logged into the account that manages the profile tells you less than nothing, because personalisation favours you.

Check properly:

  • Use a phone on cellular data, not your office wifi
  • Be logged out or in a private window
  • Search from several points across your service area, not one
  • Use the search terms customers use, not your company name

A proper grid scan measures your position from dozens of points and routinely surprises contractors who believed they ranked first. Ranking first at your own address and nowhere three miles out is the normal starting picture, not a fault.

3\u20135. Profile problems

3. Your primary category is wrong. Primary category is one of the strongest local ranking signals and broad categories dilute it. A remodeler whose qualified work is mostly kitchens should not sit under a generic contractor category. Secondary categories should extend coverage carefully rather than scatter it.

4. The profile is half filled in. Every blank field is a signal you are not sending. Complete profiles are reported 2.7 times more likely to be considered reputable. Hours, services, service area, description, attributes, recent photos \u2014 all of it feeds both ranking and whether a customer bothers calling.

5. Your profile and website disagree. An analysis of 230 local businesses found 53% had mismatched information between the two. Different hours, a different phone number, services listed in one place and not the other. It undermines trust and ranking simultaneously, and it is free to fix.

Worth twenty minutes today

Open your Google Business Profile and your website side by side. Check name, address, phone, hours, and the services list. Fix anything that disagrees. It is the least interesting task on this page and it resolves a meaningful share of visibility problems on its own \u2014 including on AI assistants, which cross-reference the two before naming anyone.

6\u20137. Suspension and eligibility

6. Your profile is suspended, or at risk. If you have genuinely vanished rather than dropped, check for a suspension notice in the profile dashboard. Reported common triggers in 2026 include address discrepancies, keyword stuffing in the business name \u2014 still surprisingly widespread \u2014 and multiple profiles created for one business using residential addresses or virtual offices.

That last practice is worth naming clearly because contractors are still being sold it. Creating extra profiles to cover more towns risks the whole cluster rather than just the extra listings, and automated audit tools will typically report every one of them as healthy right up until they disappear.

7. Your service area business setup is wrong. If customers do not visit you, Google's guidelines require hiding the street address. Displaying an address customers cannot visit is an eligibility problem. Hiding it, however, does not remove proximity from the ranking calculation \u2014 the address still anchors where you rank.

8\u20139. Website and reviews

8. Your website is not supporting the profile. The profile does most of the map pack work, but Google cross-references it against your site to verify what you do and where. A site with no service-area pages, no matching services list and no local relevance signals gives it little to confirm. This matters more now than it used to, because AI systems perform the same cross-check.

9. Review velocity has stalled. Reviews feed prominence, which is one of the three local ranking factors and the only one you can genuinely grow \u2014 proximity is fixed by your address and relevance is capped by what you actually do. Steadiness matters as much as volume: a trickle after every completed job reads as an active business, while thirty in one week reads as something else.

60%+

Reported share of local searches that end without anyone clicking through to a website at all. For a growing number of customers your Google Business Profile is your business online \u2014 which is why an incomplete one costs more than a dated website does.

Common questions

Why is my business not showing up on Google Maps?
The most common cause is proximity rather than any fault. Map pack rankings are driven heavily by how close your registered address is to the person searching, so visibility radiates outward from that address and a contractor eight miles from a suburb will struggle to appear there. Other frequent causes are checking from your own location while logged in, a broad or wrong primary category, an incomplete profile, information that disagrees with your website, a suspension, an incorrect service area business setup, weak website support, and stalled review velocity.
Does setting a bigger service area help me rank further away?
No, and this is the most expensive misunderstanding in contractor local marketing. Your service area setting tells Google where you are willing to work; it does not extend where you rank in the map pack. Proximity to the searcher remains a dominant ranking factor regardless of how wide you set that radius. What genuinely extends reach is organic service-area pages that can rank without proximity, growing prominence through reviews so you outrank closer but weaker competitors, paid channels that ignore geography, or an additional physical location.
How do I check my real Google Maps ranking?
Use a phone on cellular data rather than office wifi, log out or use a private window, and search from several different points across your service area using the terms customers actually use rather than your company name. Searching from your own premises tells you very little because you are the closest business to yourself, and searching while logged into the managing account is worse because personalisation favours you. A grid scan measuring position from dozens of points gives the accurate picture, and it commonly surprises contractors who believed they ranked first everywhere.
What gets a Google Business Profile suspended?
Reported common triggers in 2026 include address discrepancies between your profile and your verifiable records, keyword stuffing in the business name field, and creating multiple profiles for one business using residential addresses or virtual offices. That last practice carries the most risk because it can affect the entire cluster of profiles rather than just the additional ones, and automated audit tools typically report all of them as healthy right up until they are removed. If you have disappeared entirely rather than simply dropped in position, check your profile dashboard for a suspension notice first.
How long does it take to improve map pack rankings?
Profile fixes such as completing fields, correcting your primary category and resolving mismatches between your profile and website can show movement within 30 to 90 days. Building prominence through review velocity and third-party mentions is slower and compounds over three to six months. Proximity cannot be improved at all without a new location. Because the fastest fixes are also the free ones, the sensible order is always to complete and correct the profile first, then build reviews steadily, and only then consider whether the geography genuinely requires paid channels or another location.
