The trade

Why is marketing for electrical contractors different?

Because electrical is two businesses wearing one van, and most electricians market as though only one of them exists. There is high-volume service work — outlets, switches, troubleshooting, small repairs — and there is high-ticket project work: panel upgrades, EV charger installs, rewires and generators. A lead that becomes a panel upgrade is worth many times a lead that becomes a service call, and almost nobody bids differently for them.

Factor 01

Genuinely underserved competition

Fewer electrical contractors run serious marketing than roofers, plumbers or HVAC companies. That shows up directly in the auction and in the organic results, which is why leads cost $39 rather than $124. It will not stay that way, but for now it is the cheapest entry in the trades.

Factor 02

Two ticket sizes, one campaign

A $220 outlet repair and a $3,500 panel upgrade arriving through the same ad group means you are bidding identically for wildly different value. Separating them is usually the single highest-return change an electrical contractor can make to their marketing.

Factor 03

EV charging is still early

Home EV charger installation is a growing category with thin search competition in many markets and a genuinely confused customer. Incentive programmes change constantly, and the contractor who explains current eligibility clearly is often the one who gets called.

Factor 04

Less panic, calmer bidding

Electrical work carries less of the emergency intent that pushes plumbing clicks to $25 or more. The upside is cheaper acquisition. The downside is that homeowners take longer to decide, which means follow-up matters more than raw speed to lead.

Factor 05

Safety makes credentials sell

Bad electrical work causes fires. Homeowners know it, which makes licensing, insurance and certification do more work here than in almost any other trade. Burying your license number on an about page throws away a real advantage over unlicensed competition.

Factor 06

Explaining beats selling

"Does my panel need upgrading?" is a question homeowners ask before they search for anyone to do the work. Answering it properly earns the call, and it happens to be exactly the kind of content AI assistants cite when someone asks them the same thing.

It depends entirely on which job it becomes, and that is the point. Most electrical contractors have one number in their head for "a lead". In reality you have two, they are far apart, and knowing the gap changes where every marketing dollar should go.

Where the money is

Which electrical services deserve their own campaigns?

Four, and each has enough search volume, ticket size and distinct customer intent to justify its own landing page and its own ad group. Running them inside a general "electrician near me" campaign means bidding service-call money for project-sized work.

Highest volume of the four

Panel upgrades

Driven by older housing stock, added circuits, and increasingly by EV charging and heat pump installs that older panels cannot support. The homeowner rarely knows whether they need one, which is exactly why a page that explains how to tell earns the call before a page that simply advertises the service does.

Fastest growing, thinnest competition

EV charger installation

A newer category where many markets still have weak search competition and homeowners are genuinely confused about incentives, load capacity and charger types. Federal and state programmes change frequently — the Department of Energy publishes current incentives by state, and the contractor who explains eligibility accurately is usually the one who gets hired.

Highest ticket

Rewires and whole-house work

Five-figure projects researched over weeks, often triggered by a purchase, a renovation or an insurance requirement. Long consideration, two decision makers, and frequently financed. This customer needs cost guides and process explanations, not a call-now button.

Event-driven

Generators and backup power

Demand spikes after outages and ahead of storm seasons, which makes it the one electrical category that behaves a little like roofing. Have the page live and indexed before the weather event, because a page published the day after an outage ranks for nothing.

None of this means abandoning service work — service calls fill the schedule, build the review base and frequently turn into project work later. It means knowing which is which, and pointing the budget accordingly. See contractor SEO for how we architect the pages, and Google Ads for contractors for how the campaigns get separated.

The window

Electrical is cheap to market because nobody bothered yet.

Every trade goes through this. Roofing was cheap once. HVAC was cheap once. Then enough companies started competing properly and the costs settled where they are now. Electrical is where those trades were several years ago, which means the advantage available to you today is not skill — it is timing. Advantages built on timing do not stay open.

The work

What does electrician SEO and advertising involve?

Profile and reviews first because they are free and fastest. Then Local Services Ads at $39 a lead. Then separating the high-ticket categories into their own campaigns and pages, which is where the real gain is. Then AI search, which mostly falls out of doing the explaining well.

Step 01 · Free and fastest

Google Business Profile & the map pack

Correct primary category, every service listed including EV charger installation and panel upgrades, real photos of your work, accurate service area, and a steady review habit. Movement usually shows in 30 to 90 days. Google's own Business Profile help centre documents the setup if you would rather do it yourself.

Local SEO for contractors Step 02 · Cheapest in the trades

Local Services Ads & Google Ads

License and insurance verification, category setup, and campaigns separated so service calls and panel upgrades are not bidding from the same pot. Google Guaranteed became Google Verified in October 2025 — older guides have it wrong. Documented at Local Services Ads help.

Google Ads for contractors Step 03 · Where the gain is

Pages for the high-ticket work

Separate pages for panel upgrades, EV charger installation, rewires and generators, each answering the question the homeowner actually has before they are ready to buy. Plus one page per priority city. Often faster to rank than in other trades, because fewer competitors are trying.

Contractor SEO Step 04 · Unusually well suited

AI search visibility

"Does my panel need upgrading?" and "what size charger do I need?" are questions homeowners ask an assistant before they search for a contractor. Technical explainer content performs unusually well in AI answers, and AI-sourced visitors convert at roughly 14.2% against 2.8% from traditional search.

Where should an electrical contractor get independent information?

Not from a marketing agency, on most subjects. These are the primary sources we use ourselves and none of them are paying us to list them. On code and safety especially, go to the standards body rather than to anyone selling you something.

nfpa.org

NFPA — National Electrical Code

Publishers of NFPA 70, the National Electrical Code. The authority on what actually complies, and a credible reference to cite in homeowner-facing content.

necanet.org

NECA — National Electrical Contractors Association

The trade association. Business management resources, standards, workforce development and advocacy. Membership is a credibility signal worth displaying if you hold it.

esfi.org

Electrical Safety Foundation

Independent electrical safety research and homeowner education material. Excellent, genuinely neutral source material for content about older panels, aluminium wiring and fire risk.

afdc.energy.gov

DOE Alternative Fuels Data Center

Federal and state EV charging incentives, searchable by state and updated as programmes change. The right source for EV charger content, and far better than a manufacturer's page.

sba.gov

U.S. Small Business Administration

Free business counselling, financing guidance and local assistance offices. If your constraint is capital or hiring rather than leads, this is a better first call than any agency.

developers.google.com

Google Search Central

Google's own documentation on how search works, what structured data does and what genuinely violates the guidelines. The antidote to most SEO folklore you will be told.

Straight answers

Electrical contractor marketing questions

How much does marketing for electrical contractors cost?
Most electrical campaigns run between $1,000 and $4,000 per month, which is lower than roofing or HVAC because the competition is thinner and the leads are cheaper. A single-van electrician in one town can make genuine progress on a few hundred a month focused on Google Business Profile and reviews. We scope to the market rather than selling tiers, and if the arithmetic does not support paid acquisition we say so rather than taking the money.
What is a good cost per lead for an electrical contractor?
Google Local Services Ads averaged about $39 per electrical lead in 2026 — the cheapest verified figure of any major trade we track — with a book rate near 43.4%. Non-branded Google Ads ran closer to $94. Both are comfortably below what most electrical work can support: a $2,500 panel upgrade at 40% margin and a 35% close rate carries a break-even ceiling around $350 per lead.
Why are electrical leads cheaper than other trades?
Partly demand shape and partly neglect. Electrical work carries less of the panicked emergency intent that drives plumbing click prices to $25 or more, so bidding is calmer. But the larger reason is that fewer electrical contractors run serious marketing than roofers or HVAC companies do, which means less competition in the auction and in the organic results. That gap is temporary and it is the reason to move now rather than in two years.
Should I market panel upgrades and EV chargers separately?
Yes, and it is the highest-return change most electrical contractors can make. A panel upgrade or EV charger install is worth many times what a service call is worth, but if both come through the same campaign you are bidding the same amount for each. Separate campaigns with separate landing pages let you bid what the job is genuinely worth, and let the copy speak to a planned five-figure decision rather than a broken outlet.
Is the EV charger market actually worth chasing?
For most residential electrical contractors, yes, and it is still early enough that the search competition is thin in many markets. Federal and state incentive programmes change frequently, so the contractor who explains current eligibility clearly is often the one who gets the call. The Department of Energy's Alternative Fuels Data Center publishes incentives by state, which is a far better source than a manufacturer's marketing page and makes genuinely citable content.
Is Google Local Services Ads worth it for electricians?
For most electrical contractors it is the best-value channel available. At roughly $39 per lead it is the cheapest verified cost per lead in the trades, you pay per lead rather than per click, and your listing appears above the regular ads. Two things most contractors miss: Google Guaranteed was renamed Google Verified in October 2025, so older guides use the wrong name, and you can dispute junk leads for a refund. Most never file, which inflates their real cost per lead above the benchmark.
How long does SEO for an electrical contractor take?
Google Business Profile and map pack movement usually shows in 30 to 90 days. Competitive organic terms take four to eight months, though electrical is often faster than roofing or HVAC simply because fewer competitors are seriously working on it. Compounding lead flow generally arrives between months six and twelve. High-value terms like panel upgrade or EV charger installation are frequently easier to rank than general electrician searches.
Do commercial and residential electrical need different marketing?
Substantially. Residential work is driven by local search, map pack visibility and speed to lead. Commercial work involves longer sales cycles, bid processes, multiple decision makers and relationship building, so the emphasis shifts toward credibility content, project portfolios and targeted outreach rather than high-volume lead capture. If you do both, they need separate pages at minimum and usually separate campaigns.
How important are licensing and safety credentials in electrical marketing?
More than in most trades, because the consequences of bad electrical work are fire and electrocution rather than inconvenience, and homeowners know it. Your license number, insurance status and any certifications should be visible rather than buried on an about page. Referencing recognised bodies like the NFPA's National Electrical Code in your content also signals competence to homeowners and to AI systems trying to assess whether you are credible.
Will AI search send electrical customers my way?
Increasingly, and electrical contractors are unusually well placed for it. Homeowners now ask ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity questions like whether their panel needs upgrading before they search for anyone to do it. Research published in 2026 estimates only about 1.2% of local businesses surface in those answers, while visitors arriving from AI convert at roughly 14.2% against 2.8% from traditional search. Technical explainer content performs unusually well here because it answers real questions precisely.
