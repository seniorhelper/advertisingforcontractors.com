The trade

Why is marketing for a plumbing company different?

Because the buying decision takes minutes, happens at any hour, and the homeowner is contacting several plumbers simultaneously. No other trade compresses the funnel that hard. It means visibility and response speed are the same problem, and treating them separately is how plumbing companies end up paying for leads that ring out at 11pm.

Factor 01

The most urgent intent in home services

Nobody researches an emergency plumber for a fortnight. They search, they see three options, they call the first one that looks credible. That compression is why plumbing carries the highest non-branded ad costs of any trade at roughly $183 per lead — everyone bidding knows exactly how valuable that click is.

Factor 02

Emergency clicks cost three to five times more

Average plumbing cost per click sits near $8.45, but genuine emergency terms run $25 to $45. Bidding on both with one campaign and one budget quietly hands your emergency budget to low-intent daytime searches. They need separating.

Factor 03

Demand arrives outside business hours

Pipes burst at night and on weekends. If your coverage stops at five, a meaningful slice of the highest-intent demand in your market rings out. Most plumbing companies have never put a number on what that costs — the calculator below does.

Factor 04

Pay per lead beats pay per click here

With clicks that expensive and intent that urgent, Local Services Ads usually deliver a better cost per booked job than search ads. $57 a lead at 44.5% booking works out near $128 per booked job, against $183 per lead on non-branded search.

Factor 05

One job opens into several

A drain call becomes a sewer line inspection. A water heater becomes a repipe conversation. A maintenance agreement turns one customer into a decade of work. Your true lead value is almost always higher than the first invoice suggests, which means you can afford to outbid competitors only counting job one.

Factor 06

Reviews carry unusual weight

A stranger is coming into the house, at night, to fix something the homeowner does not understand, at a price they cannot verify. That combination makes review count and recency do more work in plumbing than in nearly any other trade.

Speed to lead

Why does response time matter more in plumbing than anywhere else?

Because your competitor is not the plumber ranking above you. It is whoever answers the phone first. A homeowner with an active leak works down the search results dialling until a human picks up, and the search stops the moment one does. Ranking second and answering instantly beats ranking first and going to voicemail, every single time.

The first ring

A person, not a phone tree

Someone standing in water will not navigate a menu. If the first thing they hear is "press one for service", a meaningful share hang up and dial the next result. Route emergency calls straight to a human, even if everything else in your business runs through a system.

After hours

Cover the hours when pipes actually burst

Plumbing emergencies do not respect business hours, and neither does the ad spend you are paying to generate them. Either cover the nights and weekends with an answering service that can actually dispatch, or pause the emergency campaigns when nobody is there. Paying for emergency clicks you cannot answer is the worst money in the trade.

Within minutes

Call form fills back immediately, not tomorrow

Form submissions decay faster in plumbing than in any other trade. Someone who filled in a form at 9pm has probably called two competitors by 9:15. Instant alerts to a phone, not an inbox nobody checks in the evening, and a named person responsible for calling back.

On every call

Track it, because you cannot fix what you cannot hear

Call tracking with recording tells you three things you are otherwise guessing at: which channel produced the call, whether it was answered, and what was said. Most plumbing companies discover their real problem is on the phone, not in the ad account — and they only discover it once they start listening.

2026 onward

The machines are calling now too

Google announced agentic booking for local services at I/O in May 2026, covering categories including home repair. In some cases the assistant telephones the business on the customer's behalf. If nobody answers, it moves to the next provider on the list — and unlike a homeowner, it will not feel guilty about it or try again later.

This is why we will sometimes tell a plumbing company not to buy more traffic yet. If your answer rate is 60%, doubling your leads doubles the number of people who ring out. Fix the phone first and the same spend produces materially more work — see lead generation systems for how we set that up.

Three different people

Do emergency and planned plumbing work need separate campaigns?

Yes, and mixing them is the most common structural mistake on plumbing websites. You have three distinct customers with different urgency, different budgets and different anxieties. One services page cannot speak convincingly to a burst pipe at midnight and a planned whole-house repipe.

Emergency

Minutes · night and weekend · price-insensitive

Water is going somewhere it should not. They want someone competent, now. Price barely enters the first conversation.

  • Searches: emergency plumber near me, burst pipe, no hot water, water leaking
  • Decides in: minutes, while calling several numbers
  • Wants: a human on the phone and a real arrival time
  • Clicks cost: $25–$45 on genuine emergency terms
  • What wins: answering, availability, and visible proximity

Service & replacement

Days · business hours · comparison shopped

A water heater on its way out, a slow drain, a fixture replacement. Annoying rather than urgent, so they get two or three quotes.

  • Searches: water heater replacement cost, drain cleaning, plumber plus city
  • Decides in: days, sometimes a week
  • Wants: transparent pricing, warranty, and a scheduled time that suits them
  • What wins: reviews, clear pricing signals, and easy booking
  • Where the upsell lives: maintenance agreements start here

And a third that deserves its own page entirely: repiping, sewer line and whole-house work. Five-figure projects researched over weeks, often with a spouse, frequently financed. That customer needs cost guides, material explanations and financing information — content that would be noise to someone with water on the floor. Separate pages, separate campaigns, separate copy. That split is usually the highest-return structural change a plumbing site can make; see contractor SEO for how we architect it.

We would rather tell a plumbing company to fix its phone coverage than sell it more traffic. It is the smaller sale and it is the right answer, because doubling leads into a 60% answer rate just doubles the number of homeowners who call somebody else. Get the phone right and the spend you already make starts producing what it should.

The work

What does plumbing SEO and advertising involve?

Speed to lead first, because it multiplies everything else. Then Google Business Profile, then whichever paid channel clears your break-even, then the service page architecture that ranks, then AI search — which mostly falls out of doing the rest properly.

Step 01 · Multiplies everything

Speed to lead & call handling

Call tracking per channel before a dollar of spend, instant alerts to a phone rather than an inbox, after-hours coverage that can actually dispatch, and follow-up that fires whether or not anyone remembers. In plumbing this is not the last step. It is the first one.

Lead generation systems Step 02 · Free and fastest

Google Business Profile & the map pack

Correct primary category, every service listed, real photos, accurate service area, and a review habit that produces a steady trickle rather than an old pile. Movement usually shows in 30 to 90 days. Google's own Business Profile help centre documents the setup if you would rather handle it in house.

Local SEO for contractors Step 03 · Work this week

Local Services Ads & Google Ads

License and insurance verification, category setup, emergency terms separated from daytime service so the budgets do not cannibalise each other, and the lead disputes most plumbers never file. Google Guaranteed became Google Verified in October 2025 — older guides have it wrong. Google documents it at Local Services Ads help.

Google Ads for contractors Step 04 · Compounding

Service pages that rank

Separate pages for emergency service, water heaters, drain cleaning, sewer lines, repiping and leak detection, plus one page per priority city with genuinely local content. Competitive terms take four to eight months, which is why this builds underneath paid rather than instead of it.

Contractor SEO Step 05 · The new front door

AI search visibility

Homeowners now ask ChatGPT and Gemini to recommend a plumber. Research in 2026 estimates only about 1.2% of local businesses surface in those answers, while AI-sourced visitors convert at roughly 14.2% against 2.8% from traditional search. Far fewer people, far better ones.

Where should a plumbing company get independent information?

Not from a marketing agency, on most subjects. These are primary sources we use ourselves and none of them are paying us to list them. On licensing, code and standards especially, go to the body rather than to anyone selling you something.

phccweb.org

PHCC — Plumbing-Heating-Cooling Contractors

The national trade association. Apprenticeship programmes, business management resources and advocacy. Membership is also a credibility signal worth displaying if you hold it.

iapmo.org

IAPMO

Publishers of the Uniform Plumbing Code and a major certification body. The authority on what actually meets code, rather than what a supplier tells you meets code.

epa.gov

EPA WaterSense

Efficiency standards and homeowner-facing water waste data. Genuinely useful source material if you write content about leaks, fixtures or efficiency upgrades.

sba.gov

U.S. Small Business Administration

Free business counselling, financing guidance and local assistance offices. If your constraint is capital or hiring rather than leads, this is a better first call than any agency.

support.google.com

Google Business Profile help

Authoritative documentation for the free asset that matters most in local plumbing. Everything you need to do the profile work yourself is here.

developers.google.com

Google Search Central

Google's own documentation on how search works and what genuinely violates the guidelines. The antidote to most SEO folklore you will be told.

Straight answers

Plumbing marketing questions

What do plumbing marketing companies charge?
Most plumbing campaigns run between $1,500 and $5,000 per month depending on market density and how many services you want ranking. A single-truck plumber working one town can make real progress on a few hundred a month focused on Google Business Profile and reviews. A company covering a metro against franchise competition needs the upper end. We scope to the market rather than selling tiers, and if the arithmetic does not support paid acquisition we say so rather than taking the money.
What is a good cost per lead for a plumbing company?
Google Local Services Ads averaged about $57 per plumbing lead in 2026 with a 44.5% book rate, a $1,714 average ticket and 6.85 times return on ad spend. Non-branded Google Ads averaged about $183, the highest of any trade we track, and Performance Max landed near $82. The number that actually matters is your own break-even: gross profit per job multiplied by your close rate. On a $1,714 ticket at 40% margin and a 40% close rate, that ceiling is roughly $274 — which makes a $57 lead a rounding error.
Why are plumbing Google Ads so expensive?
Because the intent is the most urgent in home services and everyone bidding knows it. Average plumbing cost per click sits around $8.45, but genuine emergency terms run $25 to $45 per click. Someone typing "emergency plumber near me" at eleven at night is not comparison shopping, and the click prices reflect that. It is also why Local Services Ads, where you pay per lead rather than per click, usually produce a better cost per booked job for plumbers than search ads do.
How fast do I need to answer a plumbing lead?
Immediately, and this matters more in plumbing than in any other trade. A homeowner with water on the floor is calling several plumbers in a row and hiring whoever picks up. A missed emergency call is rarely a delayed job — it is a permanently lost one, because by the time you call back someone else is already there. If you take one thing from this page, make it this: fixing your phone coverage is almost always cheaper than buying more traffic.
Is Google Local Services Ads worth it for plumbers?
For most plumbing companies it is the single best-value channel available. You pay per lead rather than per click, your listing sits above the regular ads, and the 2026 benchmark put plumbing at roughly $57 per lead with 44.5% booking. Two things most plumbers miss: Google Guaranteed was renamed Google Verified in October 2025, so older guides use the wrong name, and you can dispute junk leads for a refund. Most contractors never file disputes, which is exactly why their real cost per lead runs above the published figure.
How long does SEO for a plumbing company take?
Google Business Profile and map pack movement usually appears within 30 to 90 days. Competitive organic terms take four to eight months. Compounding lead flow generally arrives between months six and twelve. Because plumbing demand is immediate rather than seasonal, we almost always run paid search or Local Services Ads first to produce work this month, with organic building underneath to lower the blended cost per lead over time.
Should a plumber pay for drain and sewer leads separately?
They behave differently enough to be worth separating. Drain and sewer leads averaged about $59 on Local Services Ads in 2026, close to general plumbing, but the jobs skew toward diagnostics that can open into much larger sewer line work. Running them as their own campaign with their own landing page lets you bid appropriately and speak to the specific problem, rather than sending a homeowner with a backed-up main to a general plumbing page.
Do maintenance plans change the marketing math?
Substantially, and most plumbers underweight this. A one-off repair is worth its ticket. A homeowner on a maintenance agreement is worth that ticket repeatedly, plus the priority-service calls and the replacement work that comes later. If you sell agreements, your true break-even cost per lead is far higher than the first-job calculation suggests, which means you can profitably outbid competitors who are only counting the first invoice.
Do plumbers need separate pages for each service?
Yes, and it is one of the highest-return changes most plumbing sites can make. Water heater replacement, drain cleaning, sewer line repair, repiping, leak detection and emergency service are different searches from different customers with different urgency and budgets. A single services page cannot rank well for all of them, and it certainly cannot speak convincingly to a burst pipe and a planned repipe at the same time.
Will AI search send plumbing customers my way?
Increasingly, and very few plumbers are set up for it. Homeowners now ask ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity to recommend a plumber rather than scrolling search results. Research published in 2026 estimates only about 1.2% of local businesses surface in those answers, while visitors arriving from AI convert at roughly 14.2% against 2.8% from traditional search. The qualifying work — complete profile, consistent business details, steady reviews, structured data — overlaps almost entirely with good local SEO, so it is sequencing rather than a separate budget.
