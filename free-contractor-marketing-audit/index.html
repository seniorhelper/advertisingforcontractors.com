What does the free contractor marketing audit cover?

Six areas, twenty-four checks, and a written summary that names what to fix first and roughly what each one is worth. It's done by a person rather than generated by a scanner, which is why it takes a day instead of a minute — and why it catches the things automated tools are known to miss.

Section 01

Website & conversion

Mobile load time on cellular rather than wifi, tap-to-call placement, form friction, whether prices or ranges appear anywhere, and how your conversion rate compares to the published benchmark for your specific trade.

Section 02

Google Business Profile

Primary and secondary category selection, completeness of every field, photo recency, review velocity, Q&A, and anything that raises suspension risk — including keyword stuffing in the business name, which still appears regularly.

Section 03

Map pack visibility

Where you actually rank measured from multiple points across your service area, not from your own address. This is the section that most often surprises contractors, because ranking first in your own yard is not the same as ranking anywhere.

Section 04

Paid advertising accounts

If you're running ads: negative keyword lists, match types, branded versus non-branded reporting, Performance Max brand exclusions, and what your conversion action is actually counting. Read-only access, revocable any time.

Section 05

Tracking & attribution

Whether conversion tracking exists, whether it's counting the right things, and whether calls are attributed by channel at all. This is the most common failure we find and the one that makes every other number on your reports unreliable.

Section 06

AI search visibility

Whether AI assistants can read your business, what your robots.txt permits, what structured data exists, and how you currently surface when someone asks an assistant for a contractor in your area.

What it is not: a scanner output with a colour-coded score and a sales call attached. Automated tools measure static data well and are notoriously poor at the things that matter most here — multi-point proximity testing, suspension risk, and anything involving whether your leads actually book. Where a tool is the right instrument we use one, and then a person reads the result.

Can you audit your own contractor marketing?

Yes, and you should do these five before hiring anyone at all. They take about an hour between them, cost nothing, and find most of what actually matters. If you only ever do this and never contact us, the page has done its job.

Check 01

Time your own response

Have someone who isn't an employee submit your contact form at 4pm on a Friday. Time the reply. 78% of customers buy from whoever responds first, so this number caps the return on every channel you run — and more than half of contractors take five days or longer.

Check 02

Search from outside your postcode

Search your main service plus a suburb you want work in, on a phone that isn't on your office network. Proximity dominates map rankings, so what you see standing in your own yard tells you nothing about what a customer eight miles out sees.

Check 03

Load your site on cellular data

Not office wifi. Open your busiest service page and count seconds until it's usable. Under 2.5 seconds is the standard, 71% of your traffic is on a phone, and this one check explains a surprising share of "our ads don't work".

Check 04

Compare your profile to your website

Name, address, phone, hours, services — do they match exactly? An analysis of 230 local businesses found 53% had mismatched information between the two, and it's free to fix in about twenty minutes.

Check 05

Work out one cost per booked job

Last quarter's spend on one channel, divided by jobs actually won from it — not enquiries. If you can't produce that number, you don't yet know whether the channel works, and no report full of rankings will tell you.

Run those five and you'll have found more than most paid audits surface, because they test the things that are true rather than the things that are measurable. What to realistically expect from each channel is the natural next question once you know where you stand.

The recurring five

What goes wrong most often in contractor marketing?

Not the things contractors expect. Almost nobody's problem is that their logo is dated or their website isn't modern enough. The findings that repeat are boring, unglamorous, and expensive — and most of them were introduced by somebody who was paid to help.

What contractors think is wrong

The brief we usually get
  • "Our website looks old"
  • "We need to be on more social platforms"
  • "We're not ranking number one"
  • "We need a bigger ad budget"
  • "Our competitor has more reviews"

What's usually actually wrong

What the audit finds
  • Nobody can tell which channel produced any job
  • The phone isn't answered fast enough
  • The site is slow on the network most visitors use
  • The ad account pays for searches with no buying intent
  • The Google profile is half filled in
Finding 01

Tracking that doesn't exist, or half exists

The most common finding by a distance, and the most damaging, because it makes every other number unreliable. Sometimes there's no tracking at all; more often it's partially installed and quietly reporting bad data — counting every form submission including job applications, or missing calls entirely when calls are how most contractor work arrives. Everything downstream of this is guesswork, including any automated bidding, which optimises toward whatever it's been told to count.

Finding 02

A website that looks fine and performs badly

Most contractor sites are built as digital brochures rather than as something designed to produce enquiries. They load slowly on cellular data, bury the phone number, ask for information before offering any, and never mention price. The tell is a site that a contractor is proud of and that converts below the published benchmark for their trade — and since every channel eventually lands here, that gap taxes all of them at once.

Finding 03

A Google profile treated as an afterthought

Broad primary category chosen because it felt safe, half the fields blank, photos from two years ago, and information that contradicts the website. With more than 60% of local searches ending without a click to any website, the profile often is the customer's entire experience of your business. Complete profiles are reported 2.7 times more likely to be judged reputable, and this is free to fix.

Finding 04

Ad accounts spending on the wrong searches

Inherited Google Ads accounts almost always show the same pattern: broad match keywords, no negative keyword list, branded searches counted as campaign wins, and Performance Max running without brand exclusions. At $8 to $45 a click this is expensive, and it usually takes an afternoon rather than a rebuild to correct. The search term report tells you most of it for free.

Finding 05

Leads that arrive and then sit

The one no agency can fix for you, and the one that most often explains why a campaign "isn't working". More than half of contractors take five days or longer to respond to an enquiry while 78% of customers buy from whoever gets there first. A contractor with mediocre marketing and a two-minute response beats a contractor with excellent marketing and a two-day one, every time.

Notice how few of those cost money to fix. Three of the five are configuration and habit rather than budget, which is the main reason the audit is free — a good number of them end with us telling you to go and do something yourself rather than quoting for it.
