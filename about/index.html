Built by a contractor who got tired of paying for reports.

The origin

Why did a contractor start a marketing agency?

Because he kept hiring marketing companies that didn't care whether he made a sale.

Our founder, Zach Wennstedt, has run a bath remodeling company since 2012. Like most contractors who reach the point where word of mouth stops being enough, he went looking for help. He hired marketing companies. More than one. Each time the pattern was the same.

A confident pitch. A monthly invoice. Then reports. Pages of them — keyword positions, impressions, sessions, bounce rate, a chart with a line going up and to the right. All of it arriving on schedule, none of it answering the only question that mattered on a slow week in February: did any of this turn into a job?

Nobody ever asked him what an average bathroom remodel invoiced at. Nobody asked what percentage of estimates he closed. Nobody asked whether the leads coming in were homeowners with financing in place or people pricing a fantasy remodel they'd never book. Those questions never came up, because the answers didn't change what got billed.

The agency got paid whether the phone rang or not. That was the whole problem.

So he built the agency he'd wanted to hire. Eye To Ad Media launched in Denver in 2012, and Advertising For Contractors is the arm of it built specifically for the trades.

He still runs the remodeling business today. That matters more than it sounds like it should. It means every campaign we build gets judged by someone who knows what it costs to have a crew standing around, who has priced a job against a competitor who lowballed it, and who has personally sat with a report that said everything was going great while the schedule stayed empty.

What we believe

What does this contractor marketing company actually stand for?

Five things, and we'd rather lose a sale than break any of them. Conversions over rankings. Honest numbers over flattering ones. Owned channels over rented ones. Month-to-month over lock-in. And telling you when we're the wrong call.

01

Conversions, not rankings

A ranking is a means. A booked job is the end. We'll happily show you position movement, but if the calls aren't following it, we treat that as a problem to solve rather than a chart to celebrate. Traffic that doesn't convert is a cost, not a result.

02

Numbers you can check

Every benchmark we publish carries a source and a date. Every report ties to call tracking and conversion tracking we set up before spending a dollar on traffic. If attribution is genuinely uncertain — and sometimes it is — we say so instead of dressing a guess up as a fact.

03

Own the pipeline

Marketplace leads fill a slow week. They never build anything. We use them as a stopgap when cash flow demands it, and we spend the rest of the effort on channels you keep — your profile, your rankings, your reviews, your brand. Assets, not rent.

04

No long-term contracts

Month to month, always. We'd rather keep you because the work produces jobs than because you signed something in March. The honest caveat is that search compounds, so we tell you the realistic runway upfront rather than discovering it together in month four.

05

We'll tell you no

If your average job is small, your margins are thin and your close rate is low, paid advertising may not clear your break-even no matter who runs it. We've told contractors to put the money into a second truck instead. Losing that sale costs us less than a bad six months costs both of us.

The difference

How is AFC different from other contractor marketing services?

Most agencies serving the trades learned the trades from a case study. We learned them from a job site. That changes what we ask you, what we measure, and what we're willing to promise.

Trade-specific, not templated

Every trade gets its own strategy

A burst pipe is a five-minute decision at eleven at night. A pool build is a six-month conversation with a spouse. Roofing spikes after a hailstorm; HVAC spikes with the thermometer. Running one playbook across all of them is why so many contractors conclude marketing doesn't work. See how that plays out on our roofing marketing and plumbing marketing pages.

Break-even first

We start with your math, not our menu

Before we recommend a channel we work out what you can afford to pay for a lead — your profit per job multiplied by your close rate. That single number decides whether Local Services Ads make sense for you or whether paid search will quietly lose money on every job. Run it yourself with the break-even lead cost worksheet.

Built for AI search

We optimize for the engines answering questions

Homeowners increasingly ask ChatGPT and Gemini to recommend a contractor instead of scrolling results. Research published in 2026 estimates roughly 1.2% of local businesses surface in those answers. Most agencies haven't started on this at all. Ours is a standing part of every build.

Any budget, honestly scoped

From $50 a month to full market coverage

A one-truck painter working a single town and a roofing company covering three counties are not the same problem, and pretending otherwise with tiered packages serves the agency, not the contractor. We quote what it takes to win where you actually operate. See how pricing works.

That breadth matters here for one specific reason. Marketing that works exposes whatever is broken downstream of it. Accountability

What will you actually be able to see?

Four numbers, every month, tied to the channel that produced them: leads, phone calls, booked jobs and cost per booked job. Everything else is supporting detail.

Metric 01

Leads

Forms, calls and chats, deduplicated and tagged to source. Not sessions. Not impressions. Actual people who raised a hand.

Metric 02

Calls answered

Tracked numbers per channel, with recordings where they're useful. This is where most marketing budgets quietly die, and it's invisible without tracking.

Metric 03

Booked jobs

What actually made it onto the schedule. You tell us, we tie it back to source. It's the only number that ends the argument about whether a channel works.

Metric 04

Cost per booked job

Spend divided by jobs won, by channel. Compared against your break-even. This is the number that decides what we scale and what we cut.

Attribution is genuinely imperfect — a homeowner might see your truck, search your name, then call from a Google listing, and no tool untangles that cleanly. We'd rather hand you a real number with a caveat attached than a flattering one that's fiction. More on how we report.

About AFC

Who is Advertising For Contractors?
Advertising For Contractors, or AFC, is the trades-focused arm of Eye To Ad Media, a Denver marketing agency operating since 2012 under Search Converts LLC. It was founded by Zach Wennstedt, who has also run a bath remodeling company since 2012. AFC builds advertising and lead generation for roofing, plumbing, HVAC, electrical, solar, general contracting, painting, remodeling, pool building and fencing companies.
Why should a contractor hire a specialist agency instead of a general one?
Because trade search behavior is specific and a general agency has to learn it on your budget. Emergency plumbing searches convert within minutes; solar and pool projects take months of research. Roofing demand spikes after storms; HVAC demand follows the thermometer. Category selection in Google Business Profile alone can move a contractor several positions. A specialist starts with that knowledge instead of billing you while acquiring it.
Is AFC the same company as Eye To Ad Media?
Yes. Advertising For Contractors is the contractor-focused brand of Eye To Ad Media, which operates under Search Converts LLC in Denver, Colorado. Same team and same standards. AFC exists so contractors get advice written for the trades rather than general small business marketing content.
Do you work with contractors outside Colorado?
Yes. We are headquartered in Denver but work with contracting companies across the United States, Canada and internationally. What matters is whether we can realistically win in your market and whether the economics work for your business, not where you are on a map.
Will I work with the founder or an account manager?
Zach still works directly with clients. We deliberately keep the roster small so that stays true. You will know who is working on your account and you will be able to reach them, which is not the case at most agencies once the pitch is over.
What happens if the marketing works but we still do not book more jobs?
Then the problem is downstream of marketing and we say so. The two most common causes are response time and call handling. Leads that sit for a day go cold, and a rushed phone answer can waste a lead that cost real money to generate. We would rather help you fix the intake process than sell you more traffic pouring into the same gap.
