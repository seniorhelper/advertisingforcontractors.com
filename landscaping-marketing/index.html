What is landscaping marketing?

Landscaping marketing is the set of channels that puts a lawn care or landscape company in front of property owners at the moment they decide to hire: the Google Business Profile and map pack, service and city pages, search ads, reviews, and a follow-up system that turns quotes into signed accounts. What makes it different from most trades is that the first job is rarely the profitable part. The recurring maintenance account is. So the budget should be set against what an account is worth over its life, not against a single visit.

The trade

Why is marketing a landscaping company different from other trades?

Most contractors sell a project. A roofer replaces a roof and the relationship is mostly over for twenty years. A landscaping company is usually running three or four different businesses under one name, and each one is searched for differently, priced differently and sold in a different month.

Recurring maintenance

Weekly or biweekly mowing, edging, bed maintenance and seasonal cleanups. The ticket per visit is small, but the account renews. A property owner who is happy in May is usually still with you in October, and often the next spring too. This is the part of the business that pays for the trucks, and it is the part most marketing plans undervalue because they look only at the first invoice.

Enhancements and add-on services

Aeration, overseeding, mulch, fertilization programs, shrub trimming, irrigation startups and winterizations, leaf removal. These are mostly sold to customers you already have, which makes your existing client list the cheapest marketing channel you own. A company that never asks its maintenance customers about fall aeration is leaving the easiest revenue in the business on the table.

Design-build, installation and hardscape

Patios, retaining walls, plantings, outdoor kitchens, lighting, full landscape renovations. Higher tickets, longer decisions, more comparison shopping, and a buyer who wants to see finished work before they talk to you. The search terms are different too: people look for “paver patio installers” or “retaining wall contractor,” not “landscaping company.”

Snow and ice (in markets that have it)

For northern companies, snow contracts keep crews employed through the winter and are almost always sold in the fall, to the same commercial and residential customers who already trust you with their grounds.

The most common mistake we see is one generic “landscaping services” page and one ad campaign trying to sell all four of these at once. Google cannot tell which service you are best at, the visitor cannot find the thing they searched for, and the budget gets spent on whichever service happens to be cheapest to click on rather than the one you actually want more of.

The calendar

When should a landscaping company market each service?

Demand for lawn care and landscaping follows the weather, and the weather is local. Shift these windows to your climate: a company in Phoenix or Tampa runs a very different calendar from one in Minneapolis. The principle holds everywhere, though. Sell each service a few weeks before people need it, not on the day they need it.

Late winter

Sign the route before the grass wakes up

Renew last year’s accounts with a written agreement and an easy way to pay. Pre-sell spring cleanups. Refresh your Google Business Profile photos, hours and service list. This is when a homeowner has time to read a proposal properly, and when competitors are quietest.

Spring

Answer fast, then know when to stop

The phone rings everywhere at once. The money in spring is lost to slow callbacks, not to weak ads. Once your crews are at capacity, pull paid spend back to the services you still have room for, and keep a waitlist instead of taking work you will deliver badly.

Summer

Sell to the customers you already have

Irrigation repairs, plant replacements, lighting and enhancement work sell well to existing accounts. It is also the right time to book fall design consultations, because homeowners are using their yards and noticing what they want changed.

Fall

Aeration, cleanups, and next year’s contracts

Aeration and overseeding, leaf removal and fall planting all have a short window. In snow markets, fall is when winter contracts are signed. Design-build companies should be booking spring installs now, while the homeowner can still picture the yard.

Winter

Build the asset that pays in spring

Organic rankings take months to move, so winter is when service pages, project galleries, review requests and profile work should happen. A company that goes dark in December is invisible in March, right when it matters.

The channels

Which marketing channels work for lawn care and landscaping companies?

Not all of them, and not all at once. These are the ones we build most often for landscapers, roughly in the order we would put them in place.

Foundation

Google Business Profile and the map pack

Most local landscaping searches show a map with three businesses before any regular results. The right primary category, real service areas, steady reviews that mention the work, and fresh photos of finished properties do more here than anything else. Our local SEO for contractors page walks through the parts that move rankings.

Foundation

One page per service you want more of

Separate pages for lawn maintenance, hardscape, irrigation, lighting and the rest, each with real photos, a clear scope and the areas you cover. This is how Google learns what you do, and how a visitor looking for a paver patio lands on a page about paver patios. More on that in contractor SEO.

Speed

Search ads for the services with room

Paid search is the dial you can turn up and down. Use it for the services and neighborhoods where you have capacity, add seasonal campaigns for aeration or cleanups, and exclude searches from people looking for DIY tips or jobs. The Google Ads for contractors page covers setup.

Margin

Route-density marketing

A new account on a street you already mow is worth more than the same account across town, because the crew is already there. Yard signs with the owner’s permission, door hangers after a visible project, and neighborhood-targeted social ads all push density, which improves margin without a price increase.

Trust

Reviews that describe the work

“Great job” helps a little. “They rebuilt our retaining wall and regraded the side yard so it stopped flooding” helps a lot, because it tells the next homeowner, and Google, exactly what you do. Ask at the moment the customer is happiest, usually the day a project is finished.

Retention

Your existing customer list

Seasonal reminders to current accounts about aeration, mulch, irrigation winterization and fall cleanups are the cheapest leads you will ever get. Most landscaping companies have the list and never use it.

The sales side

How do you turn landscaping leads into signed accounts?

Marketing gets the phone to ring. Sales decides whether the call becomes a customer. Our founder, Zach Wennstedt, was a sales manager for multi-million-dollar plumbing companies and later managed national remodeling companies and their sales teams, so we look at a landscaping company’s lead handling as closely as its ads. These are the habits we build in, because a sales floor makes the same lessons obvious in every trade.

Answer while the homeowner is still looking

People asking for lawn care quotes rarely ask just one company. Research published in Harvard Business Review in 2011, covering more than a million web leads, found that firms trying to reach a lead within an hour were nearly seven times as likely to qualify it as firms that waited longer. In spring, an unanswered call from a crew truck is often a lost account. Route calls to someone who can pick up, and set an automatic text reply for anything missed. We go deeper on that in our piece on speed to lead for contractors.

Quote in writing, the same day if you can

A written proposal with a clear scope (what is included each visit, how often, what costs extra) is easier to say yes to than a number given over the phone, and it is much harder for a competitor to undercut on price alone when the homeowner can see exactly what they are getting.

Offer options, then ask for the agreement

Two or three clearly different service levels let a homeowner choose how much to buy instead of choosing whether to buy. Then ask directly for a season agreement with automatic billing. Recurring billing is what turns a lead into the multi-year account the calculator above is built around.

Follow up on a schedule, not a feeling

Most unsigned quotes are not rejections. The homeowner got busy. A short, polite follow-up on day two, day five and day twelve, by text and email, recovers work that would otherwise disappear. Put it on a calendar or in software so it happens every time, not only when the owner remembers.

Measure cost per signed account, not cost per lead

A cheap lead that never signs is expensive. Track where each signed account came from, and judge every channel by cost per account and by how long those accounts stay. If you want help setting that up, the free lead and job tracker is a simple place to start.

Own versus rent

Should a landscaping company buy leads or build its own?

Lead marketplaces have a place. They just should not be the whole plan.

Bought, shared leads

Useful for a gap in the route

  • The same homeowner is often sent to several companies at once.
  • The first conversation tends to be about price before you can explain your scope.
  • You pay for every lead whether it signs or not, and the spending never builds anything you keep.
  • Good for filling a hole in the schedule quickly when you need it.

Leads from channels you own

Slower to start, cheaper over time

  • The homeowner found you, so the call starts with your reviews and your photos, not a price list.
  • Rankings, reviews and service pages keep producing after the work is done.
  • You can aim it at the services and neighborhoods you actually want more of.
  • Every season of reviews and photos makes the next season easier.

Straight answers

Landscaping marketing questions

How much should a landscaping company spend on marketing?
There is no honest single number, because a lawn care route and a design-build firm have completely different economics. The better way to set a budget is from the inside out: work out what one signed maintenance account is worth in gross profit over the years it typically stays with you, decide what share of that you are willing to spend to win it, and multiply by your close rate. That gives you a ceiling per lead. The calculator on this page does that math with your own numbers. We build landscaping campaigns from modest monthly budgets upward, scoped to your market, with no long-term contracts.
What is a good cost per lead for lawn care or landscaping?
Good is whatever your own account value can carry. A lead that becomes a three-year weekly maintenance account can justify a far higher cost than a one-time cleanup, and a hardscape lead can justify more again. We are wary of anyone quoting a national landscaping cost-per-lead figure without naming the dataset behind it, because prices swing widely by city, season and service. Track your own cost per lead and cost per signed account by channel for one full season and you will have the only benchmark that matters.
When should a landscaping company start advertising for spring?
Earlier than most do. In markets with a real winter, the companies that sign maintenance agreements in late winter go into spring with a full route, while the companies that switch ads on when the grass turns green are bidding against everyone at the same moment and answering the phone from a mower seat. Your Google Business Profile, reviews and service pages should be in shape before the first warm weekend, and paid ads should ramp just ahead of local demand, not after it.
Should a landscaping company use Google Ads or SEO?
Usually both, for different jobs. Search ads can produce calls this week and are useful for filling a route quickly or promoting a seasonal service like aeration or fall cleanups. Local SEO and the Google Business Profile take months to build but keep producing calls without a per-click cost once you rank. A common pattern is ads in the first season while the organic side is built, then ads pulled back to the services and neighborhoods where you still need volume.
How do I get more landscaping customers in the neighborhoods I already service?
Market to the streets on your existing routes first. A new account two doors from an existing one adds revenue without adding much drive time, which is one of the few ways a landscaping company improves margin without raising prices. Practical tools include yard signs placed with permission, door hangers on the same street after a visible project, geotagged project photos on your Google Business Profile and neighborhood-targeted social ads. The work is easy; doing it every week is the hard part.
How quickly should a landscaper respond to a new lead?
As fast as you realistically can, ideally within the hour during business hours. Research published in Harvard Business Review in 2011 found that firms that tried to contact web leads within an hour were nearly seven times as likely to qualify them as firms that waited longer. Homeowners requesting lawn care quotes often contact several companies at once, and in spring the first credible reply tends to win. If the crew lead cannot answer from the field, someone in the office or an automated text-back should.
