Why we exist

Why does most contractor marketing fail?

Because most of the people selling it have never had to make payroll off a job they bid themselves.

AFC started because our founder got burned. He has run a bath remodeling company since 2012. Over the years he hired marketing companies the way most contractors do — on a promise, a slide deck and a monthly invoice. What he got back were reports full of rankings and impressions and traffic. What he never got was a straight answer to one question: did any of this turn into a sale?

Nobody asked what his average job was worth. Nobody asked what he closed. Nobody asked whether the leads were homeowners with a real budget or tire-kickers pricing a fantasy. The agencies got paid either way, and that was the whole problem — their incentive stopped at the report.

If it doesn't turn into a job, it isn't marketing. It's a bill.

So he built the agency he wanted to hire. Eye To Ad Media launched in 2012 in Denver, and Advertising For Contractors is the arm of it built specifically for the trades. He still runs the remodeling business today, which means every campaign we build gets judged by a contractor who knows what a slow February feels like.

That's the difference, and it's not a slogan. When we say we measure booked jobs instead of impressions, it's because the person who set the standard has to live with the answer. More about how AFC works, or read the longer version on Eye To Ad Media's about page.

The real question

Should you buy leads, or own them?

Buying leads from a marketplace fills a slow week. It never builds anything. You're renting access to a homeowner who has already been sold to four of your competitors, which turns the first phone call into a price fight. Industry reporting through 2026 shows contractors moving hard toward exclusive, owned lead sources for exactly that reason. Both have a place — just know which one you're buying.

Renting leads

  • The same homeowner is sold to several contractors at once
  • You compete on price before you've said a word about quality
  • Cost per lead is set by the platform and rises when they want it to
  • Stop paying and the leads stop the same day
  • You build no asset, no ranking, no brand recall
  • Disputing junk leads is on you, and most contractors never bother

Owning your pipeline

  • Every lead comes to you and only you
  • The homeowner found you, so you're the expert, not the fourth quote
  • Cost per lead drops as your rankings and review profile mature
  • The work compounds — month twelve costs less than month three
  • Your Google Business Profile, site and reviews are assets you keep
  • Branded search stays cheap forever once people know your name

Here's the honest version: if you need work in the next two weeks and you have nothing built, buy the leads. Cash flow beats theory. But start building the owned side in parallel, because a contracting business whose entire pipeline lives on someone else's platform is one pricing change away from a very bad quarter. That's the whole argument for building a lead generation system you actually control.

Straight answers

Contractor advertising questions

How much does advertising for contractors cost?
It depends on your market and your goals. We build campaigns starting around $50 per month and scale to several thousand per month for contractors competing in dense metros. A small local painter in a single town needs a very different budget than a roofing company covering three counties. We scope the plan to your goals and your budget, then grow it as the return proves out. There are no long-term contracts.
What is a good cost per lead for a contractor?
It varies by trade and channel. SearchLight Digital's February 2026 benchmark, drawn from $6.72 million in tracked spend across 888 contractors, put Google Local Services Ads at roughly $39 per lead for electrical, $51 for HVAC, $57 for plumbing and $59 for drain and sewer, with a blended average near $53 and a 43.9 percent book rate. Non-branded Google Ads run higher — roughly $124 per lead for roofing and $149 for HVAC. The number that actually matters is your own break-even, which depends on your average ticket, your margin and your close rate.
How long does contractor SEO take to work?
Google Business Profile and map pack movement usually shows in 30 to 90 days. Competitive organic terms take four to eight months. Compounding lead flow generally arrives between months six and twelve. Anyone promising page one in thirty days is selling you something. If you need the phone ringing this month, we start with paid search and build organic underneath it.
Should contractors buy leads from Angi or Thumbtack?
Shared marketplace leads can fill a slow week, but you are renting them. The same homeowner is typically sold to several contractors at once, which turns the conversation into a price fight before you have said a word. Industry reporting through 2026 shows contractors moving toward exclusive, owned lead sources for exactly this reason. Our position is simple: use marketplaces as a stopgap if you need cash flow, and build channels you own so you are not renting your pipeline forever.
Do you only work with contractors in Denver?
No. We are based in Denver, Colorado but we work with contractors across the United States and internationally. What matters is fit, not geography. We look for contracting companies that want to scale and are willing to invest consistently, in markets where we can realistically win.
What trades do you work with?
Roofing, plumbing, HVAC, electrical, solar, general contracting, painting, remodeling and home improvement, pool building and fencing. Each trade gets its own strategy because the search behavior is genuinely different. A burst pipe is an emergency search at 11pm. A pool build is a six month research cycle. The same campaign cannot serve both.
Why does AI search matter for contractors?
Homeowners increasingly ask ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity to recommend a contractor rather than scrolling search results. Research published in 2026 estimates only about 1.2 percent of local businesses are surfaced in AI recommendations. The businesses that do get cited tend to have complete Google Business Profiles, consistent business information, steady review flow and proper structured data. That work overlaps heavily with local SEO, so it is not a separate budget line — it is doing the fundamentals well enough to be machine readable.
Do you require a long-term contract?
No. Everything is month to month. We would rather keep you because the work produces jobs than because you signed something. The honest caveat: search engine optimization compounds, so leaving at month two guarantees you get nothing from it. We will tell you upfront what the realistic runway looks like before you spend anything.
