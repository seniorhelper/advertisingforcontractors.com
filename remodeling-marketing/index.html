What homeowners actually said

Why do homeowners choose one remodeler over another?

Almost never on the thing you are proudest of. The 2026 Houzz & Home Study asked homeowners who had completed renovations what part of the process could have been better. Every one of the top five answers is about being informed, not about being built for. A homeowner cannot assess your framing before they hire you — so they assess what they can.

Read that chart as a price list. Every item on it is something you can promise before the contract is signed, demonstrate on your website, and win the job with. A weekly schedule update is not a construction skill. A proposal that shows where the money goes is not a construction skill. The remodeler who advertises those things is selling directly into the gap the customer just described — and almost nobody in this trade advertises them at all, because everybody is busy advertising quality instead.

Where the job is actually won

How long is the remodeling sales cycle really?

Longer than the build in many cases. Houzz reports the planning phase now runs substantially longer than construction — in some cases close to double. Add the four to twelve weeks homeowners spend researching before they contact anyone, and most of your relationship with a customer happens before you own the job.

The honest market picture

Is remodeling actually growing in 2026?

The market is enormous and almost flat. Harvard's Joint Center for Housing Studies projects homeowner improvement spending reaching roughly $518 billion by the end of 2026, with growth easing from 2.1% mid-year to 1.6%. Meanwhile the number of firms competing for it has nearly doubled since 2000. Flat demand plus more competitors is not a market you grow in by working harder.

There is a genuinely encouraging number underneath this, though: 91% of homeowners with a planned 2026 renovation say they are going ahead with it, and 67% will keep or expand the scope. Demand is not evaporating. It is being fought over by twice as many companies as it was a generation ago, most of whom are still marketing the way they did in 2010. That is the opening.

Straight answers

Remodeling marketing questions

How much should a remodeling company spend on marketing?
Most remodeling campaigns we run land between $2,000 and $7,500 a month, and general contractor guidance puts marketing somewhere between 5% and 15% of gross revenue depending on whether you are maintaining or growing. Work backwards rather than picking a percentage: revenue target, divided by average project value, divided by close rate, multiplied by cost per lead. In remodeling that calculation is unusually reliable because the sales cycle is long enough to be measured properly.
What is a good cost per lead for a remodeling contractor?
Non-branded Google Ads for remodeling typically runs $150 to $400 per lead on clicks of roughly $8 to $18 for kitchen and bath terms. Local Services Ads land around $25 to $112, Meta between $5 and $167 depending on the offer, mature organic search $10 to $63, and third-party vendor leads $150 to $600 or more while being shared with three to five competing contractors. The blended target most published benchmarks aim at is a 5:1 return on marketing spend against a 3 to 8 week sales cycle.
Is the remodeling market actually growing in 2026?
Barely, and that is the honest answer. Harvard's Joint Center for Housing Studies projects homeowner improvement spending will grow about 2.1% mid-year, easing to 1.6% by the end of 2026, reaching roughly $518 billion. Worth noting that JCHS issued a correction in April 2026 revising those figures down from an earlier release, so anyone quoting $522 or $526 billion is citing a superseded number. The market is enormous and essentially flat, which means growth has to come from share rather than from a rising tide.
Why are homeowners choosing my competitor over me?
Often for reasons that have nothing to do with building. When the 2026 Houzz & Home Study asked homeowners what could have been better about their renovation, the top answers were schedule tracking at 44%, clearer communication at 35% and cost transparency at 26%. Nothing about the quality of the work appeared at the top of that list. Homeowners largely cannot evaluate your carpentry before they hire you, so they evaluate the things they can — how fast you replied, how clear your proposal was, and whether your pricing felt honest.
Should I publish remodeling prices on my website?
Cost guides are consistently identified as among the highest-converting content in remodeling, and publishing honest ranges is the change most contractors resist hardest. The homeowner researching a kitchen for eight weeks wants to know whether you are in their universe before they call. Ranges with the variables explained cost you the enquiries that were never going to convert and save you the site visits. There is a free builder on this page that produces the table for you.
How long is the remodeling sales cycle?
Published benchmarks put the remodeling sales cycle at roughly three to eight weeks from first contact, and homeowners commonly research for four to twelve weeks before contacting anybody at all. Houzz also reports the planning phase now runs substantially longer than construction — in some cases close to double. That matters enormously: most of the relationship happens before a tool comes out of the truck, and most contractors have no structured follow-up covering that period.
Are Angi, HomeAdvisor and Houzz Pro leads worth it?
They are expensive and shared. Third-party remodeling leads commonly run $150 to $600 or more and are typically sold to three to five contractors simultaneously, which means you are paying premium prices to enter a race you did not choose. They work as a volume filler when the schedule is thin and your response speed is genuinely fast. They are a poor foundation, because every dollar spent there builds an asset somebody else owns.
Does my remodeling website convert badly?
Higher-consideration trades like remodeling typically convert website visitors to leads at 3% to 7%, against 5% to 15% for home services broadly. Below about 3% usually indicates a page or offer problem rather than a traffic problem. The usual causes in remodeling are no price guidance, no real project photography, no statement of the work you do and do not take, and a form that asks for a phone number before it has given anything in return.
How do AI Overviews affect remodeling searches?
Substantially, because remodeling queries are exactly the informational, cost-focused questions AI answers are built to handle. Analysis by Seer Interactive found paid click-through rates falling by as much as 68% on queries where AI Overviews appear. Research published in 2026 estimates only about 1.2% of local businesses surface in AI-generated answers, while visitors arriving from AI convert at roughly 14.2% against 2.8% from traditional search. Depth and local cost specificity are what get cited — which is the same content that qualifies your buyers.
Is my problem marketing or sales?
Worth establishing before you spend anything, because the fix is completely different. If you are not getting enough enquiries, that is marketing. If enquiries arrive and quotes go out and nothing closes, that is sales — and no amount of traffic fixes it. Against a three to eight week cycle, close rate is usually the larger lever, and the honest answer is that a marketing agency is the wrong supplier for it. Sales training is a different discipline and we will point you at it rather than pretend otherwise.
