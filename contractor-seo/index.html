The shift

What does contractor SEO actually mean in 2026?

Getting into the visible parts of a results page that no longer leads with organic listings. That means the map pack first, because it sits highest and home service searches are overwhelmingly local. Then being a source the AI answer quotes. Then the organic listing itself, which still matters enormously but arrives further down the page than it used to. Any agency still selling you “position one” as the whole objective is describing a search results page that stopped existing.

Reality 01

The map pack outranks everything

Three listings with a map, sitting above the organic results, and for most home service searches it is where the clicks and calls go. It is driven by your Google Business Profile, your categories, your reviews and your proximity — not by your website's authority.

Reality 02

Traffic is a vanity number

Visits are not jobs. A contractor ranking for how to fix a leaky tap gets more traffic than one ranking for emergency plumber plus a suburb, and far fewer calls. We measure the searches attached to work you actually want.

Reality 03

Being cited beats being clicked

When an AI answers a homeowner's question, it names sources. Getting named is now a ranking outcome of its own, and roughly 1.2% of local businesses manage it — which makes it the least crowded position on the page.

Reality 04

Reviews are ranking infrastructure

Review count and velocity feed local rankings directly, which makes asking every finished customer the highest-return unpaid task in contractor marketing. It is also the one almost nobody does consistently.

Reality 05

Thin pages now actively hurt

Forty service area pages built by swapping a town name into a template were a shortcut a decade ago. Today they get discounted by search engines and skipped entirely by AI systems, and they drag on the pages that were working.

Reality 06

It compounds, or it does nothing

SEO is the only channel where month twelve costs the same as month one and produces several times more. That is the entire argument for it — and it is also why stopping at month two guarantees you get nothing at all for the money.

How we pick keywords

Which searches are actually worth ranking for?

The ones attached to a job, which is not the same as the ones with volume. We sort every keyword into four tiers by what the person searching is about to do. Most contractor websites are accidentally optimised for tier three, which is exactly why their reports show traffic climbing while the phone stays quiet.

Tier 1Needs you today
“emergency roof repair near me”  ·  “burst pipe plumber open now”

Low volume, highest conversion, most expensive to buy. Someone with a problem happening right now and no interest in comparing three quotes. Almost always a map pack and Local Services Ads fight rather than an organic one, because the searcher never scrolls.

Buy itWorth paying near your break-even. Speed to answer decides the job more than ranking does.
Tier 2Pricing a job
“roof replacement cost Denver”  ·  “how much to rewire a house”

Real intent, weeks out, and the single most under-served tier in every trade we work in. Contractors avoid publishing prices, so the results are dominated by national averages that are useless locally. Honest local ranges here both rank and pre-qualify every caller.

Own itThe best return in contractor SEO. Cheap to rank, and it filters your enquiries before you drive anywhere.
Tier 3Researching
“metal vs asphalt shingles”  ·  “do I need a permit for a fence”

High volume, months out, and where AI answers are hitting hardest — often the homeowner never clicks through at all. Still worth writing, because these are the questions AI systems assemble answers from, and being the cited source is how you get named later.

Write it, don't count on itValue it for citation and trust, not for direct leads. Judge it on assisted conversions.
Tier 4Never buying
“how to fix a leaky tap yourself”  ·  “roofing jobs hiring”

DIY guides, job seekers, students, other contractors. Genuinely large traffic numbers and close to zero commercial value. This is the tier that makes an agency report look impressive while your schedule stays empty.

Skip itAnd add these as negative keywords in paid, where they are costing you money right now.

One practical consequence worth stating plainly: a good SEO report should show some metrics going down. If we cut tier four traffic and grow tier one and two, total sessions may fall while calls rise. An agency that only ever shows you numbers going up is choosing which numbers to show you.

Later than any agency wants to admit, and then permanently. Paid search delivers leads in week one at a cost per lead that stays flat or rises with the auction. SEO delivers almost nothing for months, then produces more each month at the same spend. The work, in order

What does a contractor SEO project actually involve?

This sequence, deliberately front-loaded with the things that are free or fast. If we can move your map pack position in ninety days while the slower work compounds underneath, you get a return long before the organic rankings arrive.

Month 1 · free and fastest

Google Business Profile and reviews

Correct primary category, every service listed, real job photos, service areas that match where you actually work, and a review request that fires after every completed job. This moves faster than anything else available to a contractor and it costs nothing but consistency. Detail is on the local SEO page.

Month 1 · before content

Fix what is leaking

Site speed on a phone, because your customer is standing in a yard on mobile data. Click-to-call that works. Forms that submit. Tracking installed before a dollar goes anywhere. There is no point ranking a page that cannot convert, and technical faults are usually the cheapest fix on the whole account.

Months 1–3

One real page per service

Not a services list. A page per money service, written to what a buyer asks: what it costs, how long it takes, what goes wrong, what you actually do. These are the pages that rank for tier one and tier two, and most contractor sites collapse all of them into a single paragraph on the homepage.

Months 2–5

Service areas, honestly scoped

A page for each area you genuinely serve, naming neighbourhoods, local conditions, permit rules and jobs completed there. Five real pages outperform forty templated ones, and the templated approach now drags down the pages that were working rather than just failing quietly on its own.

Months 3–8

Cost content and the questions nobody answers

Local price ranges, timelines, permit realities, what makes a quote go up. This is tier two territory, it is nearly unclaimed in every trade, and it is the content AI systems assemble answers from — which is currently the least crowded position on the results page. See AI search optimization.

Ongoing

Report on jobs, not rankings

Calls and forms attributed to source, and where possible booked work. A ranking report is not a result, it is an input. If tier four traffic falls while tier one calls rise, we will show you both and explain why the first number went the right way by going down.

When not to buy SEO

If you can't fund six months of it, don't start.

SEO compounds, which is a polite way of saying it gives you almost nothing early. A contractor who spends three months on it and stops has bought nothing at all — the money would have produced more work as paid search. We would rather tell you that on the first call than take three months of fees and hand you a ranking report. If the runway isn't there yet, start with ads and come back to this when it is.

Own or rent

Should a contractor choose SEO or Google Ads?

Both, in sequence, and the order depends entirely on your cash position. Paid search is a tap: turn it on, leads arrive this week, turn it off and they stop the same afternoon. SEO is a well: months of digging, then water at a cost that stops rising. The mistake is treating them as competing options rather than as two different time horizons.

Paid search

Immediate, controllable, rented

The right first move for most contractors with an empty schedule. We set it up regularly and it does exactly what it says.

  • Leads within days, which matters when crews are idle
  • You control pacing, geography and which services get budget
  • Cost per lead is knowable up front and scales predictably
  • Rises with auction pressure and you absorb all of it
  • Stops producing the afternoon the card gets declined
  • Builds no asset — month twelve starts from zero again

Search engine optimization

Slow, compounding, yours

Rankings, map position, reviews and content. Months to build, then it keeps producing without a per-lead charge.

  • Cost per lead falls as the asset matures instead of rising
  • The map pack sits above the ads for most home service searches
  • Content earns AI citations that paid search cannot buy
  • Branded search stays cheap forever once people know your name
  • Four to eight months before it carries real weight
  • Worthless if you stop at month two — genuinely, entirely worthless

Straight answers

Contractor SEO questions

How much does contractor SEO cost?
Industry guidance in 2026 puts typical contractor SEO budgets between $2,500 and $7,500 per month, and that range is roughly right for a company competing across a dense metro. It is not the whole picture though. A one-truck operation working a single town can make genuine progress from around $50 a month by fixing its Google Business Profile, building a real review habit and publishing a handful of honest service pages. We scope to the market rather than selling tiers, and there are no long-term contracts. The honest caveat is that search engine optimization compounds, so a budget you cannot sustain for six months is a budget better spent on paid search instead.
How long does SEO take to work for a contractor?
Google Business Profile and map pack movement usually shows within 30 to 90 days, because that work is largely about completeness and consistency rather than authority. Competitive organic rankings take four to eight months. Compounding lead flow, where the channel starts producing more than it costs every month without further investment, generally arrives somewhere between months six and twelve. Anyone promising page one in thirty days is either talking about a keyword nobody searches or selling you something. If you need the phone ringing this month, start with paid search and build organic underneath it.
Is SEO or Google Ads better for contractors?
They answer different questions, so the comparison is usually framed wrong. Paid search buys you position today and stops the afternoon you stop paying. SEO buys you an asset that costs the same each month while producing more, which means its cost per lead falls as it matures rather than rising with the auction. For most contractors the right answer is both, in sequence: paid search for cash flow now, SEO underneath it for the year after. The calculator on this page shows roughly when the owned channel overtakes the rented one at your own numbers.
Do AI Overviews mean SEO is dead for contractors?
No, but they have changed what winning looks like. Analysis by Seer Interactive found paid click-through rates falling by as much as 68% on queries where AI Overviews appear, and organic results are pushed further down the page. What has not changed is that the AI answer is assembled from sources, and being one of those sources is now a ranking outcome in its own right. Research published in 2026 estimates only about 1.2% of local businesses surface in AI-generated answers, while visitors arriving from AI convert at roughly 14.2% against 2.8% from traditional search. Fewer clicks, better clicks, and a much smaller set of winners.
What keywords should a contractor target?
The ones attached to jobs you want, not the ones with the biggest search volume. A term like emergency roof repair near me converts at a completely different rate to roof types explained, even though the second gets more traffic. We sort keywords into four tiers by intent: someone who needs you now, someone pricing a job, someone researching, and someone who will never buy. Most contractor websites are accidentally optimised for the third tier, which is why they report traffic going up while the phone stays quiet.
How many service area pages should a contractor have?
As many as you have genuine service areas, and not one more. The failure mode is generating forty near-identical pages by swapping a town name into a template, which Google has been discounting for years and which now also reads as low value to AI systems. A real service area page names the neighbourhoods, references local conditions, permit rules or housing stock, and shows work you have actually completed there. Five real pages outperform forty thin ones, and they are far less likely to drag down the rest of the site.
Can I do contractor SEO myself?
A meaningful amount of it, yes, and we would rather say so. Claiming and completing your Google Business Profile, choosing the right primary category, adding real job photos, and asking every finished customer for a review will move you further in ninety days than most paid campaigns at a small budget. Those are the highest-return hours available to a contractor and they cost nothing but attention. What is harder to do yourself is the technical work, the site architecture and the sustained content, mostly because it competes with running jobs.
What is the difference between SEO and local SEO for contractors?
Local SEO is the part that decides whether you appear in the map pack, the three business listings shown with a map above the regular results. It is driven by your Google Business Profile, proximity, categories, reviews and consistent business information. Broader SEO decides whether your website ranks in the regular results below that. For most contractors the map pack matters more, because home service searches are overwhelmingly local and the map sits higher on the page. The two overlap heavily, so we build them together rather than as separate projects.
