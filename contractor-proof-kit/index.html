Why doesn’t “licensed and insured” win the job anymore?

Because every contractor says it, and nobody can check it. A homeowner comparing three websites sees the same three words on all of them. What separates you is a fact they can verify: a license number with the agency that issued it, the coverage types you carry, a warranty in writing, and reviews that live on someone else's site. Consumer advice from the Better Business Bureau tells homeowners to check exactly these things before they hire, so make it easy for them.

01

Checkable beats impressive

“Fully licensed” is a claim. “Florida license CBC… (your number), check it on the DBPR site” is proof. The second one feels confident because it invites checking.

02

Specific beats vague

Name the coverage: general liability, workers' comp, bonded. Name the warranty length and what it covers. Vague promises read like a sales pitch; specifics read like a business.

03

True beats everything

Never claim a license, bond or rating you don't hold. Advertising claims have to be backed up (the FTC's 2024 rule on fake reviews shows how seriously regulators take it), and a homeowner who catches one stretch stops believing the rest.

How to find your official lookup page

Search your state's name plus “contractor license lookup” and use the result on a .gov site (or the board's own domain). Examples:

  • California CSLB license check
  • Florida DBPR license search
  • Minnesota DLI license lookup

Some trades are licensed by your city or county instead of the state. In Colorado, for example, the state licenses plumbers and electricians while general contractors are licensed locally. Links checked November 7, 2026.

Questions contractors ask about proof

Should I put my contractor license number on my website?
Yes, if your trade is licensed where you work. A number plus the name of the agency that issued it lets a homeowner confirm you on the official lookup in under a minute, which a bare “licensed and insured” line can't do. Some states also require the license number in advertising, so check your board's rules.
What if my trade isn't licensed by the state?
Many trades are licensed by a city or county instead, and some aren't licensed at all. Show what does exist: your local license or registration, your business registration with the Secretary of State, your insurance and your written warranty. Never imply a state license you don't hold.
Should I post my certificate of insurance online?
Name the coverage types on the site and say a certificate is available on request, then have your insurance agent send it directly to the customer. A PDF you post yourself can be old or edited, and homeowners and property managers increasingly know that.
Does a trust badge help me rank on Google?
There's no evidence a badge by itself moves rankings. What it can do is help the people who already found you decide to call. Checkable facts, clear business details and real reviews are also what Google's own guidance on helpful content points toward.
Is the information I type into the Proof Kit saved?
No. The kit runs entirely in your browser. Nothing you type is sent to us or stored, and closing the tab clears it.
