    Reading your result

    What is a good website score for a contractor?

    Ninety or above is excellent and rare. Eighty to eighty-nine is strong. Seventy to seventy-nine is workable but leaking. Sixty to sixty-nine means the site is costing you jobs your marketing already paid for. Below sixty means fixing the website will produce more work than increasing your ad budget would. The bands are deliberately harsh, because a scorecard that tells everyone they are doing fine is useless.

    The bottleneck

    Why is your website the most important asset you own?

    Because every channel eventually sends people there. Search results, the map pack, Google Ads, a truck wrap, a yard sign, a referral checking you out before they call — all of it funnels to the same handful of pages. If those pages don't convert, everything upstream is paying to lose customers more efficiently.

    Run the arithmetic. Five hundred visitors a month at a 1% conversion rate is five leads. The same five hundred at 4% is twenty. Identical traffic, identical spend, four times the work. Buying more visitors to solve a conversion problem is the most expensive mistake in contractor marketing, and it's the one agencies quietly benefit from.

    There's a second reason, and it surprises most contractors. Industry data published in 2026 puts phone leads converting at roughly 46% against 8–12% for form submissions, with 37% of phone leads closing on the first call. If your number isn't visible in the header of every page and tappable on a phone, you're pushing your best-converting lead type into your worst-converting channel.

    The method

    How is the score weighted?

    By effect on booked jobs, not by how many questions sit in each section. A missing phone number in the header costs you more work than a missing FAQ page, so it carries more weight. The five sections total exactly 100 points.

    28 points · 6 checks

    Conversion path

    The heaviest section, because it multiplies every other channel. Phone visibility, clear positioning, short forms, service pages, and a call to action someone can find without scrolling.

    22 points · 5 checks

    Local search

    Google Business Profile, review count and velocity, consistent business details, and service area coverage. This is where home services jobs are actually won.

    20 points each

    Speed and trust

    Load time, mobile rendering, security and tracking on one side. Real photos, visible reviews, licensing and a named human on the other. Both are table stakes and both are commonly missing.

    The last 10 points cover AI readiness — whether ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity can actually read and cite your site. Research published in 2026 estimates only about 1.2% of local businesses get surfaced in AI recommendations, and the work overlaps almost entirely with good local SEO. It's a small section because it's a small amount of extra effort, not because it doesn't matter. More on AI search optimization for contractors.

    Before you spend more

    Doubling your conversion rate doubles your leads for free.

    Going from 1% to 2% has exactly the same effect on booked work as doubling your ad budget — except it costs nothing every month afterwards, and it makes every dollar you eventually do spend work twice as hard. That is the whole argument for scoring the site first.

    About the scorecard

    What is a good website score for a contractor?
    There are five bands and they are deliberately harsh. Ninety or above is excellent and rare. Eighty to eighty-nine is strong, with a few gaps worth closing. Seventy to seventy-nine is workable but leaking a meaningful share of the people who reach your site. Sixty to sixty-nine means the site is costing you jobs your marketing already paid to generate. Below sixty means fixing the website will produce more work than increasing your ad budget would, and you should do that first.
    What if I don't know the answer to a question?
    Answer "not sure" and the scorecard treats it as a gap, because from a customer's point of view an unverified item behaves the same as a missing one. Those answers are flagged separately in your report so you know which items to go and check rather than fix blindly. If you email yourself the report we can verify the technical ones for you.
    Does a low score mean I need a new website?
    Usually not. Most low scores come from a handful of missing items rather than a fundamentally broken site, and a large share of the fix list costs nothing but an afternoon. Adding a tappable phone number to your header, putting real job photos on the homepage and finishing your Google Business Profile will move most contractor sites twenty points without a rebuild.
    Why does the phone number question carry so many points?
    Because phone leads convert several times better than form submissions in home services, and a large share of homeowners are searching on a phone with an urgent problem. Published 2026 data puts phone lead conversion around 46% against 8 to 12% for forms, with 37% of phone leads closing on the first call. Hiding the number below the fold routes your best converting channel into your worst.
    How is the score weighted?
    By effect on booked jobs rather than by how many questions sit in each section. Conversion path carries 28 points because it multiplies every other channel you run. Local search carries 22 because that is where home services jobs are actually won. Speed and trust carry 20 each as table stakes. AI readiness carries 10 — a small section because it is a small amount of extra effort on top of good local SEO, not because it does not matter. The five sections total exactly 100.
    Is this the same as a Google PageSpeed score?
    No, and they measure different things. PageSpeed measures load performance and nothing else. This scorecard covers load speed as one section of five, then looks at whether the site actually converts the visitors it loads — phone visibility, trust signals, service pages, local search presence and whether AI assistants can read you. A site can score well on PageSpeed and still lose almost every visitor, which is the situation this tool is built to catch.
